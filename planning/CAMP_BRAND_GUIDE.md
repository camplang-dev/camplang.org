# Camp Brand Guide

**Version:** 1.1  
**Brand:** Camp programming language  
**Theme:** Modern-rustic

---

## 1. Brand Essence

Camp is a modern programming language with a visual and verbal identity rooted in warmth, durability, and technical clarity.

The brand brings together two seemingly different qualities:

- **Modern:** precise, capable, clean, forward-looking, and thoughtfully engineered
- **Rustic:** warm, grounded, durable, familiar, and welcoming

The result should feel contemporary first, with rustic influence expressed through color, material cues, and metaphor rather than nostalgia or ornament.

### Core idea

> **Modern tools gathered around durable foundations.**

The campfire represents useful energy, transformation, shared knowledge, and a place where different generations of technology can meet.

---

## 2. Name and Casing

The canonical spelling of the language is **Camp**.

Use **Camp** in prose, headings, documentation titles, presentations, and general brand communication.

Correct:

- Camp is a modern programming language.
- Built with Camp.
- The Camp compiler.
- Learn Camp.

The logo wordmark is an intentional exception and appears as:

> **CAMP**

The all-uppercase wordmark is a graphic treatment, not the canonical spelling of the language name.

Avoid using **CAMP** in ordinary prose unless reproducing the logo, discussing the wordmark itself, or working within a context that requires all-uppercase typography.

---

## 3. Brand Personality

Camp should feel:

- Modern
- Warm
- Pragmatic
- Dependable
- Clear
- Capable
- Grounded
- Welcoming
- Technically credible
- Quietly confident

Camp should not feel:

- Antiquated
- Faux-vintage
- Corporate and sterile
- Aggressively futuristic
- Cute or childish
- Rugged for its own sake
- Outdoorsy in a commercial or recreational sense
- Overly nostalgic
- Decorative at the expense of clarity

---

## 4. The Modern-Rustic Balance

The Camp identity works through contrast.

| Rustic cue | Modern expression |
|---|---|
| Campfire | Clean geometric symbol |
| Warmth | Precise layout |
| Natural materials | Flat digital surfaces |
| Durable foundations | Contemporary tooling |
| Shared gathering place | Interoperability and community |
| Tradition | Forward-looking design |
| Ember and wood tones | Restrained interface palette |

Rustic influence should be subtle. Camp should never resemble a camping brand, heritage goods company, brewery, cabin rental service, or outdoor apparel label.

Modernity should be equally restrained. Camp should not rely on neon color, glass effects, cyberpunk imagery, or generic blue-and-black developer branding.

## 5. Logo

### Primary mark

The Camp logo consists of:

1. A stylized orange flame
2. Two crossed dark-brown logs
3. The word **CAMP** in bold uppercase lettering

The mark is vertically stacked, centered, compact, and visually balanced.

### Symbolism

- **Flame:** energy, transformation, compilation, and active development
- **Inner flame:** focus, clarity, and a living technical core
- **Crossed logs:** durable foundations, established systems, and structural support
- **Campfire:** a shared place where modern and legacy technologies can meet

### Visual character

The logo is rustic in subject but modern in execution.

Its shapes should remain:

- Smooth
- Minimal
- Geometric
- Symmetrical
- Cleanly rendered
- Free of unnecessary detail

### Logo variants

Use the complete mark for:

- Brand introductions
- Cover pages
- Posters
- Presentation title slides
- Repository social cards
- Major announcements

Use the campfire symbol alone for:

- Favicons
- Avatars
- App icons
- Small interface marks
- Compact badges

### Clear space

Leave clear space around the logo equal to at least the height of the letter **C** in the wordmark.

### Minimum size

Recommended minimum sizes:

- Complete logo, digital: **120 px wide**
- Symbol only, digital: **24 px wide**
- Complete logo, print: **30 mm wide**
- Symbol only, print: **8 mm wide**

### Backgrounds

Preferred backgrounds:

- Warm white
- Ivory
- Light warm gray
- Deep brown, with an appropriately reversed wordmark

Avoid busy imagery, low-contrast backgrounds, and strong competing colors.

### Incorrect logo use

Do not:

- Stretch, skew, rotate, or compress the mark
- Rearrange the flame, logs, or wordmark
- Add outlines, glows, bevels, or heavy shadows
- Apply distressed or wood-grain textures
- Place the logo inside a rustic badge or crest
- Recolor the flame with cool or neon hues
- Turn the logo into a cartoon or literal camp scene
- Replace the wordmark with an unrelated type style

## 6. Color Palette

The palette is warm, restrained, and grounded.

The values below are derived from the current raster logo and should be treated as a practical starting point until final vector artwork establishes exact production values.

### Primary colors

| Name | Hex | Character |
|---|---:|---|
| Ember Orange | `#CB5A1D` | Active, energetic, distinctive |
| Flame Gold | `#DC7924` | Warm, bright, secondary flame tone |
| Camp Brown | `#523828` | Material, grounded, supportive |
| Charcoal Brown | `#2F1E15` | Serious, precise, softer than black |
| Warm White | `#FAF8F6` | Open, calm, contemporary |
| Ash | `#E2D8D3` | Quiet, structural, neutral |
| Weathered Taupe | `#B29B8C` | Subdued, natural, secondary |

### Color behavior

**Ember Orange** is the main accent. It should feel like a spark rather than a flood. Use it to draw attention, create rhythm, and bring energy to an otherwise neutral composition.

**Charcoal Brown** is the primary dark tone. It replaces pure black in most branded materials and gives the identity a warmer, more distinctive voice.

**Warm White** is the preferred background. It provides a softer canvas than stark digital white without appearing aged or cream-heavy.

**Ash and Weathered Taupe** support hierarchy and atmosphere. They should remain quiet and secondary.

Documentation and API reference surfaces should be more restrained than brand-forward pages. They may lean closer to neutral technical-reference styling, with orange used mainly for links, active navigation, small callouts, and focus states. Long-form reading and reference lookup should not feel saturated, themed, or visually tiring.

### Color proportion

A typical composition should be dominated by warm neutrals and dark brown, with orange used selectively.

A useful visual ratio is:

- 70% warm white and pale neutrals
- 20% charcoal and brown
- 10% orange and flame accents

This is a compositional guide rather than a strict formula.

For documentation and API reference pages, the orange proportion should usually be lower than the general brand ratio. These pages should be dominated by readable text, code, tables, quiet navigation, and pale structural borders.

### Gradients

Gradients may be used sparingly within flame imagery or large atmospheric fields.

Preferred direction:

- Burnt orange to flame gold
- Deep brown to charcoal brown
- Warm white to pale ash

Avoid glossy, metallic, rainbow, or high-saturation gradients.

## 7. Typography

Camp typography should be modern, highly legible, and quietly distinctive.

Rustic character should come from the overall composition, not from novelty lettering.

### Primary type style

Use a clean geometric or humanist sans-serif with:

- Strong uppercase forms
- Clear lowercase letterforms
- Moderate width
- Balanced proportions
- A friendly but serious tone
- Excellent readability at multiple sizes

The type should feel engineered without becoming cold.

### Monospace type

Use a contemporary monospace for code and technical identifiers.

It should provide:

- Clear punctuation
- Strong distinction between similar characters
- Comfortable rhythm
- A neutral, refined appearance
- No exaggerated retro-terminal styling

### Wordmark treatment

The logo uses **CAMP** in uppercase with broad letter spacing and a heavy geometric weight.

This treatment is specific to the wordmark. It should not be copied indiscriminately across all headings.

### Typographic hierarchy

- Display headings: bold, concise, spacious
- Section headings: strong but restrained
- Body text: readable, calm, generous in line spacing
- Captions and labels: compact, precise, visually quiet
- Code and technical identifiers: monospace

Reference typography should prioritize scanability over brand expression. API pages, tables, signatures, parameter lists, and long language-guide chapters should feel like durable technical documentation that a reader can keep open for hours.

### Avoid

- Western display fonts
- Slab serifs selected only to look rustic
- Distressed lettering
- Stencil fonts
- Faux-handwritten type
- Pixel fonts
- Overly playful rounded fonts
- Monospace type used for all brand copy

---

## 8. Composition and Layout

Camp layouts should feel ordered, open, and purposeful.

### Core traits

- Strong alignment
- Clear hierarchy
- Generous negative space
- Balanced asymmetry
- Simple geometric structure
- Calm visual pacing
- Limited decorative elements

### Shape language

Use:

- Rounded but not bubbly corners
- Broad rectangular panels
- Circular or arched accents inspired by the flame
- Crossed or intersecting lines used sparingly
- Simple blocks that suggest structural foundations

Avoid:

- Ornamental frames
- Faux-wood borders
- Rope, badges, shields, or seals
- Excessively soft pill-shaped layouts
- Angular science-fiction motifs
- Visual clutter

### Borders and depth

Prefer thin, warm-gray borders over heavy shadows.

Depth should be subtle and functional. Surfaces may feel lightly layered, but never glossy or floating in an exaggerated way.

Documentation and API layouts should be especially quiet. Sidebars, tables,
signature blocks, and navigation groups should use simple spacing, thin
borders, restrained backgrounds, and minimal accent color. They should feel
boring in the best sense: stable, predictable, readable, and hard to tire of.

### Texture

Texture is optional and should remain nearly subliminal.

Appropriate references include:

- Uncoated paper
- Fine canvas
- Soft charcoal grain
- Subtle wood fibers

Texture should support warmth without creating a vintage, handmade, or weathered appearance.

## 9. Iconography

Icons should share the logo's balance of organic warmth and geometric control.

### Style

- Simple silhouettes
- Consistent stroke weight
- Slightly softened corners
- Minimal internal detail
- Clear recognition at small sizes
- Flat or nearly flat rendering

### Visual metaphors

Appropriate motifs include:

- Flame
- Spark
- Foundation blocks
- Intersections
- Layers
- Bridges
- Paths
- Rings or gathering points

Literal camping imagery should be rare.

Avoid tents, mountains, axes, pine trees, lanterns, or trail badges unless the context specifically calls for them and the execution remains abstract.

---

## 10. Illustration

Illustration should feel conceptual rather than scenic.

### Preferred direction

- Clean vector forms
- Limited color palette
- Technical diagrams softened by organic curves
- Abstract systems converging around a shared center
- Layers representing generations of platforms
- Structured compositions with generous negative space

### Visual themes

Useful themes include:

- Different systems gathering around one language
- Modern and established platforms connected by a shared tool
- Transformation represented through heat, light, or layered forms
- Durable structures supporting new growth
- Multiple paths converging into one clear workflow

### Avoid

- Cartoon camp scenes
- Forest landscapes
- Lumberjack or frontier imagery
- Photorealistic fire
- Fantasy flames
- Retro-computing pastiche
- Mascot-driven illustration
- Busy isometric scenes

---

## 11. Photography and Material Cues

Photography is optional. When used, it should reinforce craft, durability, and thoughtful engineering.

### Suitable subjects

- Carefully photographed legacy hardware
- Contemporary and older systems shown together
- Metal, paper, wood, and matte technical materials
- Warm workspaces
- Close details of tools and components
- Human collaboration shown naturally and without stock-photo exaggeration

### Lighting

Preferred lighting is:

- Warm but neutral
- Soft
- Directional
- Low in visual drama
- Rich in material detail

Avoid:

- Orange-tinted lifestyle photography
- Generic campfire scenes
- Outdoor adventure imagery
- Heavy sepia grading
- Dramatic sparks and flames
- Blue neon server-room clichés

---

## 12. Motion

Motion should feel smooth, controlled, and intentional.

### Appropriate motion

- Gentle fades
- Short directional transitions
- Subtle changes in scale
- Simple line or layer movement
- Restrained flame-inspired motion for special brand moments

### Flame motion

When the flame is animated, it should move as a clean abstract form rather than as a realistic or cartoon fire.

The motion should be:

- Slow
- Subtle
- Looping without distraction
- Smooth rather than flickering
- Used sparingly

Avoid sparks, smoke, crackling effects, or constant movement across ordinary brand materials.

---

## 13. Voice and Tone

Camp speaks with the warmth of a good collaborator and the precision of an experienced engineer.

### Voice qualities

#### Clear

Use direct language and uncomplicated sentence structure.

#### Warm

Sound welcoming and human without becoming overly casual.

#### Pragmatic

Favor usefulness, specificity, and grounded claims.

#### Respectful

Treat established systems and programming traditions as valid participants, not as punchlines.

#### Confident

State ideas plainly without hype, swagger, or exaggerated promises.

#### Modern

Use contemporary language and avoid faux-rustic phrasing.

### Tone examples

Preferred:

- “A modern language with room for legacy.”
- “Built for new ideas and durable systems.”
- “Modern tooling, established targets.”
- “Bring the systems that still matter.”
- “Clear language design. Broad target support.”

Avoid:

- “Blaze a trail through your codebase.”
- “Throw another legacy platform on the fire.”
- “The hottest new language.”
- “Coding from the digital frontier.”
- “Where rugged developers roam.”

The campfire metaphor is most effective when it remains implicit or appears only occasionally.

## 15. Brand Tensions to Preserve

Every Camp expression should maintain several productive tensions.

### Warm, not soft

The brand should feel approachable without appearing unserious or overly gentle.

### Modern, not sterile

The brand should feel current without adopting the impersonal look of generic enterprise software.

### Rustic, not nostalgic

The brand may suggest material warmth and durable traditions, but it should not imitate historical design styles.

### Technical, not intimidating

The brand should communicate expertise without becoming dense, aggressive, or exclusionary.

### Friendly, not playful

Camp should feel welcoming without relying on jokes, mascots, cartoons, or novelty.

### Distinctive, not decorative

The visual identity should be recognizable through disciplined use of shape, color, and tone rather than excessive ornament.

## 16. Visual Direction Summary

A Camp-branded composition should typically include:

- A warm-white or lightly tinted neutral field
- Charcoal-brown typography
- Ember-orange accents
- Clean geometric structure
- Generous spacing
- Restrained rounding
- Crisp technical content
- Very subtle material warmth
- The campfire symbol used with confidence and restraint

Reference surfaces may use an even quieter interpretation of the brand: mostly
neutral structure, excellent typography, clear code presentation, and only
occasional ember-orange accents.

The final impression should be:

> **A contemporary programming language with warmth, durable foundations, and room for multiple generations of technology.**

---

## 17. Compact Creative Brief

> Create visual and verbal materials for Camp, a modern programming language that welcomes both contemporary and legacy targets. The canonical spelling is “Camp,” while the logo wordmark appears as “CAMP.” Use a modern-rustic identity centered on a clean campfire symbol: an ember-orange flame above dark-brown crossed logs, paired with bold uppercase geometric lettering. The brand should feel technically credible, warm, pragmatic, durable, and welcoming. Favor warm neutral backgrounds, charcoal-brown typography, restrained orange accents, modern sans-serif type, refined monospace type for code, precise layouts, generous negative space, and subtle material cues. Documentation and API reference pages should be quieter and more technical, using the brand as a restrained accent rather than a theme. Avoid outdoor-brand imagery, frontier aesthetics, distressed vintage styling, cartoon fire motifs, retro-computing clichés, and cold corporate visuals.

---

## 18. Canonical Brand Summary

**Camp is modern in capability, rustic in warmth, and grounded in durable computing traditions.**

Its campfire identity is not a reference to outdoor recreation. It is a metaphor for useful energy, lasting foundations, and a shared place where modern and legacy technologies can meet.
